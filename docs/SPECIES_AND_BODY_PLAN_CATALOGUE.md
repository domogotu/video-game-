# Lethal Absorption — Species and Body-Plan Catalogue

**Status:** 100 proposed original base-species concepts. Design catalogue only; no species, models, animations, stats, or gameplay are claimed implemented.

## Purpose and player transformation rules

These 100 species are a starting roster of organisms the player may encounter, study, absorb, and potentially use as biological source material for evolving their own body. They are not 100 fixed character classes, and the player does not simply select a species and instantly gain its complete form or abilities.

The player begins as a simple amoeba. Through absorption and evolution, they can acquire compatible traits, organs, movement methods, defenses, senses, and body-plan features. Depending on what has been absorbed, researched, unlocked, and made compatible, the player may evolve toward a two-legged, four-legged, serpentine, aquatic, aerial, many-limbed, amorphous, or stranger form. Mixed anatomy and hybrid configurations are core goals. A source species may provide only an eligible trait rather than its entire body plan.

**Species record rule:** Every developed species needs habitat, ecological role, diet/prey, behavior, body plan, senses, movement, attacks/defenses, eligible absorption yields, mutation conditions, weaknesses/counters, stage relevance, variant hooks, and Codex confidence. These concise roster descriptions are design starting points, not final balance specifications.

## How species become player evolution options

1. **Encounter:** the player must personally locate and encounter the relevant organism; a shared Codex entry does not count as personal encounter.
2. **Observe and survive:** gather evidence about its habitat, behavior, attacks, defenses, and weaknesses.
3. **Hunt, interact, or acquire a sample:** the outcome depends on the species, world rules, and the player's actions.
4. **Absorb or analyze eligible material:** yields may include biomass, genetic data, organs, senses, locomotion, defense, or ability research; not every specimen yields every trait.
5. **Resolve compatibility:** body plan, tissue, organ space, energy, stage, and other explicit rules determine what can be installed or evolved.
6. **Develop the trait:** acquisition, installation, mastery, and transformation are separate progression steps.
7. **Record the result:** Codex records observed versus confirmed traits, provenance, risks, variants, and combination discoveries.

## Hybridization and open-ended body plans

- The 100 entries are a starting roster, not a cap on future species, variants, hybrids, or player builds.
- Player anatomy may combine eligible traits from multiple sources: for example, a serpentine torso, climbing limbs, wing-derived glide membranes, and an electric organ, if compatibility rules support the combination.
- The player may be bipedal, quadrupedal, serpentine, aquatic, aerial, many-limbed, amorphous, crystalline, or a hybrid; a humanoid body is never mandatory.
- A species' entire appearance is not copied by default. The player chooses among unlocked compatible body-plan options and may retain a distinct personal silhouette.
- Variants should change behavior, ecology, abilities, and absorption opportunities—not just color.
- Unverified combinations remain hypotheses until tested in-world and entered in the Codex.
- Species-specific transformation is a later evolution milestone, not a replacement for the player's accumulated anatomy and history.
- Sapient and sentient entities need explicit world, narrative, and ethical rules; they should not be treated as interchangeable with ordinary wildlife.
- Cosmic species and godlike traits remain late-stage concepts, gated by survival, research, compatibility, and progression.

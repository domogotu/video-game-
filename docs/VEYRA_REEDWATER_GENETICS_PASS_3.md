## 6. Player skill and evolution boundaries

- **Current Dart:** interpret local water vectors; no default aquatic respiration.
- **Reedstalker:** observe concealed approach; learned hunting tactics are not germline modules.
- **Bark Lantern:** identify signaling and crevice habitats; no automatic bioluminescence.
- **Gloam Moth:** study airflow and landing windows; flight requires anatomy, energy, compatible evolution and training.
- The Slime begins mostly blind and manually selects available chemical/vibration sensory views; newly studied eyes, wings or light organs are not instantly equipped.
- Every ability requires separate compatibility, unlock, practice, mastery, cost, failure state, and counterplay.

## 7. Ecological feedback and seasonal simulation requirements

1. Current Dart egg survival depends on oxygen, silt, flood flow and connected nursery water. Pool disconnection prevents unlimited replenishment.
2. Reedstalker nesting and shoreline hunting depend on reed cover and flood level; predator density responds to prey, not spawn timers alone.
3. Bark Lantern requires humid deadwood and fungal food. Clearing deadwood reduces local shelter and may remove propagule-transport opportunities.
4. Gloam Moth recruitment requires a viable larval host and pupation cover; adult pollination only occurs through compatible flower visitation and pollen transfer.
5. Restoration and damage can coexist: moth adults may support flowering plants while larvae consume foliage; predators can alter grazing pressure indirectly.
6. All ecological effects must be spatially local, resource-conserving, and reversible only through supported recovery mechanisms.

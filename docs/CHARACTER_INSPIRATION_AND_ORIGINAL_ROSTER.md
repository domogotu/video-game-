# Lethal Absorption — Character Inspiration and Original Character Framework

> **Status:** Proposed design specification. This document does not mean characters, assets, or systems are implemented. All named examples below are original archetype concepts, not existing franchise characters.

## Purpose

Lethal Absorption should let players encounter, study, battle, ally with, and evolve alongside characters that evoke the range and dramatic appeal of the games and anime listed in the Game Design Bible. The goal is to bring in broad qualities—distinctive combat identities, memorable transformations, signature techniques, rivalries, strange worlds, tactical gadgets, magic, cosmic scale, and emotional character arcs—while creating original people, species, factions, visual language, stories, and abilities.

**Design rule:** use references to identify the kind of experience desired, never as a template for copying a recognizable character. Do not reuse franchise character names, likenesses, costumes, logos, signature dialogue, exact backstories, distinctive move names, or near-identical combinations of traits.

## Inspiration-to-originality pipeline

For each reference, record:
1. **Desired feeling or design principle** — what makes the reference appealing at a broad level.
2. **Abstract mechanics** — e.g., escalating transformations, expressive combo combat, tactical gadgets, spell customization, exploration, or survival.
3. **Original premise** — a new motivation, culture, species, conflict, and role in Lethal Absorption.
4. **Original expression** — new silhouette, proportions, palette, costume, animation, voice, names, visual effects, and move design.
5. **Game function** — mentor, rival, boss, faction leader, ally, roaming hunter, trader, research subject, or late-game cosmic threat.
6. **Absorption/evolution relevance** — eligible traits or techniques, research requirements, compatibility, cost, limitations, and counterplay.
7. **Review gate** — compare the result against its inspirations and redesign if it remains recognizably too close to one specific character or franchise.

A character can be inspired by several broad influences at once. Do not make a one-to-one reskin or a thinly disguised mash-up.

## Original character archetypes to develop

These are starting concepts for a future roster; names and details can change during production.

### 1. The Threshold Duelist
A wandering fighter whose body reconfigures in response to an opponent's tactics. Their strongest techniques require reading patterns and accepting temporary anatomical weaknesses. Potential role: rival, mentor, or recurring boss.

### 2. The Relic Symbiote
A researcher fused with a living mineral colony. It creates temporary tools, shields, and sensory organs, but every configuration consumes stored mass and can be disrupted by resonance. Potential role: quest-giver, crafting specialist, or contested research target.

### 3. The Storm Choir
A multi-bodied aerial species that coordinates through vibration and electrical pulses. Its combat identity is positioning and team synchronization rather than a single overpowering attack. Potential role: faction, ally, or group boss.

### 4. The Hollow Sovereign
An ancient ruler whose apparent immortality comes from distributed biological anchors, not simple resurrection. Players must discover and understand those anchors; the character's culture and motives determine whether conflict can be avoided. Potential role: planetary story antagonist.

### 5. The Wayfinder Organism
A living explorer adapted to vacuum, radiation, and stellar navigation. Its anatomy functions as propulsion and protection, reinforcing biological spaceflight instead of introducing conventional spaceships. Potential role: late-stage mentor, rival, or cosmic guide.

### 6. The Unfinished Apex
A rapidly evolving creature whose mutations are powerful but unstable. It can become a rival shaped by the player's actions and the world ecology. Potential role: recurring hunt, emergent boss, or alternate progression mirror.

### 7. The Archive-Born Envoy
A sapient being who carries shared discovery records but cannot automatically reproduce another organism's mastery. It tests whether the player understands the difference between knowing a trait and earning compatible use of it. Potential role: Codex guide or faction negotiator.

### 8. The Planetary Gardener
A colossal ecological engineer that reshapes habitats over time. Defeating it may permanently alter a biome; cooperating with it may unlock restoration, symbiosis, or planetary-conquest alternatives if those are later approved. Potential role: world event or non-hostile apex.

### 9. The Counter-Evolver
A hunter who studies the player's visible behavior and develops plausible countermeasures over repeated encounters. It must rely on observed evidence, not hidden access to the player's build. Potential role: rival NPC and test of varied tactics.

### 10. The Last Form Artisan
A master of body-plan design who teaches players to build unusual hybrid anatomies. Their own form is unmistakable but not a fixed class template. Potential role: anatomy mentor and advanced customization unlock.

These archetypes are design seeds, not final character canon. Avoid making every character a combatant or every encounter an absorption opportunity; characters may have goals, boundaries, alliances, cultures, and nonviolent outcomes.

## Character record standard

Every major original character should have:
- Unique name, species/culture, silhouette, proportions, palette, materials, animation language, voice direction, and visual effects.
- Motivation, values, fear, personal history, relationships, faction ties, and change over time.
- Narrative role, first encounter, escalation path, possible alliances, and consequences of defeat or survival.
- Combat rhythm, signature technique family, movement style, defense, weaknesses, tells, counters, phase changes, and team interactions.
- Absorbable material/traits/techniques (if any), evidence requirements, eligibility, compatibility, yield, mutation risk, and mastery distinction.
- Ecological/world effects, witness and investigation behavior, and how knowledge of the character spreads through the Codex.
- Multiplayer fairness, accessibility/readability, exploit resistance, acceptance criteria, and test cases.
- Inspiration notes that describe broad goals and demonstrate how the final design differs from any reference.

## Player characters versus authored characters

- **Player characters:** assembled from eligible anatomy, traits, abilities, forms, and evolution choices. Preset body plans can be starting points, not permanent classes.
- **Authored NPCs:** have original motivations, recognizable combat identities, relationships, and encounter-specific behavior.
- **Bosses and rivals:** can demonstrate advanced build combinations and transformation drama, but their signature mechanics must have understandable tells, limits, and counterplay.
- **Faction and companion characters:** support diplomacy, research, crafting, exploration, team abilities, and persistent world changes, not only combat.
- **Legacy NPCs:** must continue to follow the existing privacy, snapshot, permanent-death, and no-resurrection rules.

## Absorption boundaries

Encountering a character does not automatically grant their full identity or complete power set. Discovery, sample acquisition, analysis, compatibility, mutation selection, installation, and mastery are distinct steps. Unique powers may require a personal feat, research chain, rare material, or compatible anatomy. Sapient characters have agency and rights in the world fiction; they should not be treated as interchangeable loot containers. Any lethal PvP absorption remains subject to the project's explicit high-stakes PvP and fairness rules.

## Acceptance criteria

A character concept is ready for detailed design when:
1. It is recognizably original rather than a direct copy or thinly disguised reskin.
2. Its silhouette, motive, power logic, movement, weakness, and story role are distinguishable from the rest of the roster.
3. Its abilities have defined costs, limits, counters, and interactions.
4. Any absorption rewards are specific, eligible, and compatible—not automatic full character copying.
5. Its behavior respects what it could actually observe or learn about the player.
6. Its role remains meaningful even if the player cannot or chooses not to absorb from it.
7. Its implementation status is recorded separately from design approval.

## Open decisions

- Final size of the authored NPC, rival, companion, and boss roster.
- Which archetypes appear in the opening region versus later planetary/cosmic stages.
- Whether any authored characters can become companions, and the boundaries of companion control.
- How much named-character content is authored versus generated from original modular templates.
- The review process for reference distance, narrative sensitivity, and originality.
- Which characters and absorption rewards are required for the main story versus optional discovery.

**Current status:** Proposed design framework. No character, art asset, animation, voice, or in-game behavior is claimed to be implemented.


## Additional original route seeds: shadow, martial ascendant, and goblin

These three concepts were added to capture the broad experiences requested from *Solo Leveling*, *Dragon Ball*, and *Re:Monster*. They remain original design directions rather than franchise-character adaptations.

### 11. The Umbral Successor

A disregarded survivor who learns to read residual imprints and bind a small number of temporary echoes after a difficult personal trial. Its identity is command discipline, tactical formations, and the burden of maintaining a growing retinue—not an unlimited army. Potential role: secret player evolution route, recurring rival, or original late-game NPC. Counterplay includes disruption, command overload, upkeep, range limits, and opponents who target the controller rather than the echoes.

### 12. The Limit-Breaker Disciple

A martial artist whose techniques develop through practice, pattern-reading, energy discipline, and increasingly dangerous rivals. Transformations require distinct achievements and bring specific risks, recovery costs, and altered combat options. Potential role: player route, mentor, rival, or tournament/boss character. The character must not reproduce an existing alien warrior lineage, transformation look, named attack, costume, or iconic scene.

### 13. The Scrap-Crowned Goblin

A small goblin survivor who turns scavenging, careful consumption, field crafting, observation, and social strategy into an advanced adaptive lineage. The character's strengths come from preparation and compatible incremental gains rather than automatically stealing every ability from defeated targets. Potential role: player evolution route, companion, faction leader, or rival. A meaningful solo path and group path are both candidates; whether both are required remains open.

The detailed action gates and working ability families are maintained in [Shadow Sovereign, Limit-Breaker, and Goblin Evolution Routes](SHADOW_SOVEREIGN_LIMIT_BREAKER_GOBLIN_ROUTES.md). Names are placeholders subject to originality review. These concepts are not final canon and have not been implemented.



### 14. The Mythic Staff Ascendant

A wandering staff fighter whose strength comes from footwork, reach, feints, aerial traversal, counters, and trials that test restraint as well as force. The route can be a playable discipline and can support original mentors, rivals, and mythic trial bosses. Its staff and transformation options have explicit costs, recovery, and counterplay. The broad inspiration is mythic staff-fighter agility and transformation—not a copied Sun Wukong depiction or any specific game's version. Whether the route is a starting option or a hidden unlock remains open.

Detailed route design: [Mythic Staff Ascendant (Wukong-Inspired)](WUKONG_INSPIRED_MYTHIC_STAFF_ASCENDANT.md). Design-only; not implemented.

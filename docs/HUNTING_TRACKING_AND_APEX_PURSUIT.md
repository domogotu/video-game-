# Hunting, Tracking, and Apex Pursuit System

**Project:** Lethal Absorption  
**Status:** Design specification — not implemented or tested  
**Design inspiration:** The tension, tracking, stealth, and hunter-versus-prey fantasy associated with Predator games; all creatures, lore, abilities, visual identity, and rules must remain original to Lethal Absorption.

## 1. Core fantasy

Hunting is a complete gameplay activity, not simply attacking a creature after finding it. The player can become a patient tracker, stealth ambusher, relentless pursuer, environmental trapper, sensory specialist, or direct apex challenger. Depending on the encounter, the player may be the hunter—or discover that something more dangerous has been hunting them.

Hunting connects the living ecosystem, creature AI, perception and investigation, diet, absorption, research, mutation, mastery, equipment, and the narrator's adaptive field guidance.

## 1A. Core gameplay identity: action RPG first, hunting as a connected system

**Lethal Absorption is not a hunting simulator. It is an evolutionary action RPG in which hunting is one major way to discover threats, gather knowledge, earn resources, and develop the player's body.** The moment-to-moment experience should combine the demanding, readable melee and boss-fight feel associated with *Black Myth: Wukong* with the open-world exploration, training, cooking, character progression, mobility, and spectacular power growth associated with *Dragon Ball Z: Kakarot*. These are high-level references only; the game must use original characters, abilities, visual language, story, and encounters.

### The intended play rhythm

1. **Explore freely:** travel through a reactive world, discover landmarks, gather resources, meet characters, and stumble onto unusual activity without always accepting a formal hunt.
2. **Notice or investigate:** clues can reveal a creature, rival, rare resource, hidden route, ecological change, or dangerous encounter. Players may investigate, ignore it, or return later.
3. **Prepare the character:** select abilities, traits, equipment, and a meal where useful. Preparation offers advantages but should not become mandatory busywork before every fight.
4. **Fight with action-RPG skill:** use responsive movement, dodges, spacing, timing, combos, counters, powers, transformations, and anatomy-specific attacks. Major enemies need readable tells, meaningful openings, and distinct attack patterns—not only oversized health bars.
5. **Adapt during combat:** identify patterns, switch tactics, combine compatible powers, exploit terrain, and choose when to press the attack or disengage. Enemy counters must be fair and learnable.
6. **Resolve the hunt and evolve:** outcomes may provide nourishment, materials, mastery, research, access, or eligible evolution opportunities. A kill alone never guarantees every trait or reward.
7. **Return to the wider adventure:** continue exploration, training, cooking, story, rivalries, social encounters, and other objectives. Hunting should feed the larger RPG loop rather than replace it.

### Combat and exploration principles

- **Combat is active and expressive.** Hunting can end in a direct boss fight, but tracking should create context and tactical choices—not force every encounter into slow stealth.
- **Multiple valid approaches:** ambush, ranged pressure, staff/weapon-style techniques where equipped, close-range combos, powers, traps, evasion, environmental tactics, or direct challenge. Which options are available depends on the character's anatomy, abilities, equipment, and mastery.
- **Bosses are skill checks, not stat checks alone.** Distinct phases, telegraphed attacks, punish windows, movement challenges, and opportunities to learn should reward mastery while allowing different builds to succeed.
- **Exploration stays rewarding between hunts.** Hidden areas, resources, training opportunities, secrets, NPC activity, and environmental storytelling must stand on their own.
- **Progression changes how the player plays.** New anatomy and abilities should unlock different movement, defense, combos, senses, and strategies—not merely larger damage numbers.
- **Do not import exact licensed expression.** Borrow broad design lessons, not protected characters, signature animation sequences, exact move lists, story beats, or visual designs.
- **Scope remains staged.** These are long-term identity goals. They do not expand the frozen opening vertical slice, which remains limited to its already-defined basic movement, gathering/absorption, dodge, attack, growth, and one trait choice.


## 2. The hunt loop

1. **Find signs:** Discover tracks, disturbed plants, shed scales, broken branches, nests, territorial markings, scent traces, blood or other species-appropriate fluids, heat, vibration, sound, energy residue, or prey behavior.
2. **Identify the trail:** Use current senses, environmental clues, journal knowledge, and prior research to infer what passed through and how recently.
3. **Observe:** Learn the target's route, feeding behavior, senses, vulnerabilities, social group, territory, and likely escape or attack response.
4. **Prepare:** Choose a route, approach, suitable equipment or traits, food effects, environmental advantage, distraction, camouflage, or ambush location.
5. **Engage or disengage:** Stalk, ambush, chase, flush from cover, bait with a decoy, outmaneuver, fight directly, or abandon the hunt.
6. **Resolve the encounter:** The target may be killed, escaped, wounded, driven off, studied, aided, or otherwise resolved according to its species and the world's rules.
7. **Use the outcome:** Gain appropriate nourishment, material, verified knowledge, a research clue, mastery, territory access, or a compatible absorption/mutation opportunity.
8. **Persist the consequence:** The creature, nearby ecosystem, journal, and any ongoing rivalry can reflect what happened.

Not every hunt ends in a kill. A successful hunt can mean gathering information, surviving a dangerous predator, driving a threat away, or locating a rare species.

## 3. Tracking and sensory play

Tracking clues are species- and environment-dependent. No single vision mode should reveal every creature automatically.

### Track types
- **Physical traces:** footprints, claw marks, broken foliage, displaced soil, shed parts, nesting material, feeding remains.
- **Biological traces:** scent, fluids, hair-like fibers, spores, pheromones, or species-specific signals.
- **Environmental traces:** heat, disturbed water, vibrations, frost, scorch marks, pressure changes, energy residue, or damaged structures.
- **Behavioral evidence:** frightened prey, silence in a normally active habitat, alarm calls, abandoned feeding grounds, territorial displays, or a changing migration route.
- **Social information:** calls, signals, pack responses, warnings, and credible reports from intelligent allies or NPCs.

### Sense rules
- Senses have ranges, costs, blind spots, interference, and counterplay.
- Weather, terrain, water, wind, age of trail, target speed, and target behavior can affect clue strength.
- Tracks can be confused by rain, crowds, competing species, deliberate misdirection, or overlapping routes.
- Scent masking, moving through water, taking a different route, using decoys, or disturbing the environment can reduce tracking confidence where biologically plausible.
- Senses should be legible to players. Avoid an always-on supernatural scan that removes the need to observe.
- The player can unlock or evolve different tracking methods; anatomy and body plan may change how a sense works.

## 4. Stealth and approach choices

Possible approaches include:
- **Stalk:** Move slowly, use cover, manage noise and visibility, and close distance without triggering alert behavior.
- **High-ground or vertical approach:** Use climbing, gliding, jumping, or other anatomy-appropriate movement.
- **Camouflage:** Blend with a suitable environment or reduce a specific sensory signature; camouflage is not universal invisibility.
- **Distraction:** Create sound, scent, movement, energy, or other credible bait away from the intended route.
- **Ambush:** Choose a terrain bottleneck, prey route, nest approach, or escape path; the target may still notice clues.
- **Pursuit:** Follow a fleeing target while managing stamina, terrain, and the risk of entering a rival's territory.
- **Direct challenge:** Confront the target openly, accepting that it may call allies, defend its territory, or counterattack.
- **Nonlethal observation:** Stay concealed and gather knowledge without forcing combat.

Each approach should have trade-offs. Camouflage can fail against scent or vibration; chasing can exhaust the player; an ambush can be detected; a direct attack can create noise and attract a stronger hunter.

## 5. Dynamic target and prey behavior

Targets should not be passive loot containers. Their response depends on species, awareness, health, social behavior, confidence, and local ecology.

Possible responses:
- Continue routine behavior when unaware.
- Become suspicious and investigate a clue.
- Freeze, hide, flee, or warn others.
- Herd or defend vulnerable young.
- Call a pack, guardian, or nearby ally if that behavior is appropriate.
- Lead pursuers through difficult terrain or toward a defended area.
- Fight when cornered or when territory, young, or food is threatened.
- Abandon a resource or territory after repeated pressure.
- Remember a credible encounter and change future behavior within bounded limits.

Predators should hunt suitable prey, but can fail, be injured, lose a trail, compete with rivals, or abandon a costly chase. Prey should have viable escape behaviors rather than merely waiting to be consumed. The ecosystem must remain bounded and understandable.

## 6. The player can be hunted

At appropriate progression stages, dangerous NPC-controlled creatures can track the player using the same fair evidence model. The player can notice warning signs, counter-track, misdirect, hide, flee, prepare an ambush, or deliberately turn the hunt around.

A dangerous hunter should communicate its presence through clues and behavior. It must not teleport to the player, know exact coordinates without a credible sensory source, or bypass tutorial/safe-zone protection. New players must be protected from unfair high-tier pursuit.

A hunt can become a tense contest of information: each side leaves evidence, tests the other's reactions, and attempts to break contact or gain advantage.

## 7. Special hunts and rare creatures

Some hunts are routine ecosystem encounters; others are extended investigations around unusual individuals.

Special hunts may involve:
- An Alpha whose territory is marked by missing prey and distinctive calls.
- A rare variant whose tracks reveal an unusual anatomy or elemental adaptation.
- A migratory creature that can be found only by following a changing trail.
- A predator that has learned to avoid the player's common approach.
- An anomalous creature whose clues require multiple senses or environmental conditions.
- A named adversary with persistent history and a unique reason for hunting or avoiding the player.

Rare encounters should be discoverable through observation, clues, habitat knowledge, behavior, and world-state conditions. Random chance can contribute, but essential progression and required knowledge must have reliable routes. Do not guarantee a rare mutation or reward simply because the player found the creature.

## 8. Rewards: food, knowledge, materials, and evolution

Rewards depend on species, player actions, world rules, and the encounter outcome.

- **Nourishment:** Eligible food sources can affect condition and growth according to existing diet rules.
- **Research:** Observing or surviving a hunt can reveal behavior, sensory limits, habitat, and compatibility clues.
- **Materials:** Shed material, environmental residue, or permitted remains may support crafting or research.
- **Mastery:** Repeated successful tracking, evasion, ambush, or counter-hunting can improve relevant mastery.
- **Evolution clues:** A hunt may suggest a possible trait or synthesis, but eligibility and compatibility still apply.
- **Ecological consequence:** Removing, sparing, displacing, or helping a creature can alter local behavior and resources.
- **Social consequence:** Intelligent species may respond to the player's conduct; sapient beings are not treated as ordinary ingredients or interchangeable loot.

Absorption is governed by the game's established consent/eligibility, compatibility, discovery, and stabilization rules. A kill must not automatically grant every trait the target possessed.

## 9. Narrator's adaptive hunting guidance

The narrator reacts to actual field observations and the player's current build. It acts as an in-world guide to becoming more capable, while its hidden agenda is to cultivate a body it hopes to occupy.

Example hint progression:
- **Notice:** “The smaller creatures have stopped crossing this clearing.”
- **Question:** “Something may be controlling this route. Look at the broken bark above your eye line.”
- **Hypothesis:** “The marks are too high for the local grazers. A climber—or something much larger—may be using the ridge.”
- **Lead:** After more evidence, the narrator may suggest a nearby vantage point, a time to observe, or a sense useful for following the trail.
- **Confirmation:** Once the player finds and studies the hunter, the narrator can connect its behavior to a known research or evolution opportunity.

The narrator must distinguish verified knowledge from hypotheses and speculation. It may subtly encourage a hunt because the result could improve the player's body, but hints must be grounded in clues the player can plausibly access. It must not reveal every secret through omniscience or guarantee a rare outcome.

Its guidance can adapt to the player's playstyle:
- A player who frequently rushes may be encouraged to observe tracks and use cover.
- A player with strong sensory traits may receive deeper interpretations of weak clues.
- A player struggling against a predator may receive optional suggestions about stamina, terrain, defensive traits, or disengagement.
- A player who discovers an unusual diet or adaptation may be directed toward compatible research, without being forced into one build.

Hint frequency should be configurable where feasible. The player can ignore advice and still progress.

## 10. Team Hunts and Asymmetric Monster Pursuit

The hunting concept also supports a coordinated team-versus-monster experience inspired by the broad structure of *Evolve*: one side cooperates to track a dangerous evolving creature, while the target uses stealth, feeding, growth, and its own combat choices to survive. The mode and all creatures, roles, names, visuals, and lore remain original to Lethal Absorption.

### A. Hunting team

A small squad of players or, where appropriate, NPC allies can combine different strengths rather than all using the same tracking tool.

Possible role specializations emerge from equipment, traits, and player choices rather than mandatory fixed classes:
- **Tracker:** reads trails, predicts routes, and distinguishes fresh evidence from old signs.
- **Scout:** searches ahead, marks hazards, and locates likely feeding or resting areas.
- **Controller:** deploys temporary barriers, snares, distractions, or terrain denial where the setting supports them.
- **Support / field specialist:** helps allies recover, manages supplies, identifies weaknesses, or provides countermeasures.
- **Striker:** applies pressure and punishes openings, but may be less effective at tracking or support.

These are flexible team functions, not locked classes. Players should be able to change their build and contribute in more than one way. Communication and complementary tools should matter more than raw damage stacking.

### B. The evolving monster

A player-controlled or NPC-controlled monster has a separate survival loop:
1. Find food or other valid growth sources.
2. Move through the environment while managing the evidence it leaves.
3. Learn the hunters' position from credible cues such as sound, line of sight, traps, marked trails, or environmental disturbances.
4. Decide whether to hide, misdirect, flee, feed, defend a resource, or attack.
5. Gain growth and choose from eligible evolutionary improvements.
6. Reassess whether to continue evading or risk a confrontation.

Growth should create strategic trade-offs, not a simple timer that automatically decides the winner. Feeding may reveal the monster's location or leave evidence; remaining hidden may delay growth; fighting early may be risky but can disrupt the team. The hunters must have counterplay, and the monster must have meaningful options besides running until a timer expires.

### C. Hunt stages and escalation

A match or encounter can move through readable phases:
- **Search:** the team collects clues and estimates the monster's route.
- **Contact:** hunters confirm the target's presence or force it to react.
- **Pressure:** the team coordinates tracking, containment, and attacks while the monster uses escape routes or counters.
- **Adaptation:** the monster may gain eligible growth milestones; hunters respond to observed changes.
- **Showdown or escape:** either side can create a decisive opportunity, but the outcome depends on play and rules rather than a scripted win.

Phase changes should follow actual evidence and encounter state. The game must not reveal exact coordinates for free, spawn the monster directly in front of the team, or force a final arena without a clear mode rule.

### D. Win conditions and fair asymmetry

Different playlists can support different objectives:
- **Elimination hunt:** defeat the target within the match's rules.
- **Survival hunt:** the monster survives or escapes until the objective window ends; the team wins by stopping or containing it.
- **Objective hunt:** the team protects, studies, or captures a non-sapient target using a specifically designed nonlethal objective, while the monster can disrupt and escape.
- **Ecological contract:** the team tracks a dangerous NPC creature in the persistent world, with optional objectives for observation, defense, or containment.

A player-controlled monster in a competitive match must follow explicit match defeat and respawn rules. This mode cannot permanently kill a player character or permanently remove the opponent's inventory, anatomy, discoveries, specimens, or progression. This preserves the project's existing rule that PvP never causes permanent character death or permanent loss. Persistent-world permadeath remains restricted to eligible NPC-controlled threats under the established rules.

### E. Rewards and progression

Competitive hunts can award a protected reward pool such as disclosed currency, mastery, rank/season progress, cosmetics, research clues, crafting materials, or consumables. The reward rules must be visible before the match. Winning must not allow players to steal an opponent's build, anatomy, inventory, or permanent discoveries. Avoid repeat-match farming and collusion with participation rules, matchmaking, and reward limits.

Hunting a creature in the persistent world can award knowledge or ecological consequences even when it escapes. Player-versus-player monster matches should not grant guaranteed rare traits or exclusive mandatory progression simply for winning.

### F. Matchmaking and balance

- Match players within reasonable progression and capability bands, or normalize selected match-only values.
- The monster must be dangerous without relying on unreadable burst damage or unavoidable control.
- The team must be effective through coordination, not by stacking one unstoppable damage combo.
- Every major monster power needs a tell, a limitation, and at least one counter or response.
- Monster growth must be observable and earned, with catch-up and pacing rules still open for later testing.
- The team should be able to recover from a missed clue; a single early mistake must not automatically decide the match.
- Prevent queue abuse, intentional feeding, farming, griefing, and reward manipulation.
- Competitive rules and persistent-world rules must be clearly separated in the UI and design.

### G. Solo and cooperative accessibility

The feature should not require every player to participate in competitive multiplayer. NPC hunting teams, solo contracts, co-op hunts against AI monsters, and optional asymmetrical player matches are possible extensions. The full mode is long-term multiplayer scope and must not expand the opening vertical slice.

## 11. Connection to existing systems

- **Perception, Investigation, and Adaptive Hunter AI:** Hunting uses evidence-driven awareness, last-known-position investigation, bounded memory, and fair pursuit.
- **Alpha and Special Creatures:** Special hunts use ecological signs, meaningful differences, and consequences rather than inflated health alone.
- **Creature Ecosystem:** Predators hunt appropriate prey, prey evade, and food availability influences movement and diet.
- **Evolution and Absorption:** Hunting creates opportunities for nourishment, research, and compatible adaptations without guaranteed trait theft.
- **Cooking, Equipment, Stats, and Build Synergy:** Food, gear, stamina, senses, and traits may change preparation and approach.
- **Rivalry and Adaptive Adversaries:** Persistent hunters can learn from credible encounters and develop bounded countermeasures.
- **Endgame Narrator Reveal:** Helpful hunting advice is also part of the consciousness's effort to test and cultivate its chosen vessel.

## 11. Scope boundaries

The full hunting ecosystem is a long-term system. It must not be pulled into the frozen opening vertical slice as a full feature set.

The initial slice remains focused on teaching movement, absorption/gathering, dodge, basic attack, growth, and one meaningful trait choice in a compact environment. If hunting is later introduced in a bounded follow-up milestone, use one readable predator/prey relationship, a small number of physical clues, a simple observe-or-escape choice, and no full rare-hunt catalogue.

Do not implement all sensory types, special hunts, advanced traps, full ecosystem simulation, or the player-as-prey endgame before the foundational encounter is proven.

## 12. Design questions for later

1. Which senses and tracking tools are available to the initial playable body, and which must be discovered or evolved?
2. Which approach actions are universal, and which require anatomy, traits, gear, or mastery?
3. How are tracking confidence and trail age communicated without clutter?
4. Which hunt outcomes can produce research without a kill?
5. Which rare hunts are procedural, handcrafted, or hybrid?
6. How much player guidance is the default, and how can players reduce it?
7. What are the exact reward, persistence, and simulation budgets?

## Status

This document records a design direction only. Hunting mechanics, UI, AI, rewards, sensory effects, and narrator hint triggers have not been implemented or playtested. Numerical values, content catalogue, and exact unlock rules remain open for design and future validation.

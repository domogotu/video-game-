## 1. Glass-Eyed Pouncer — LA-PLN-AMP-001

**Source:** [species dossier](species/LA-PLN-AMP-001_GLASS_EYED_POUNCER.md). Small quadrupedal ambush predator of rocky woodland and scrub. **Reproduction:** concealed litter in dry den; gestation, parent roles, fertility, and heritability mechanisms unresolved.

| Candidate module | Dossier-grounded expression | Prerequisite | Cost, weakness, or constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| GEN.SEN.POUNCER_MOTION_CONTRAST_EYES | Paired image-forming eyes detect low-light contrast and motion | Functional eye/visual processing system | Glare, opaque cover, lighting changes |
| GEN.LOC.POUNCER_EXPLOSIVE_HINDQUARTERS | Short powerful pounce | Four-limb support, spinal control, stable foothold | Poor long chase, landing recovery |
| GEN.MOR.POUNCER_GRIPPING_FORECLAWS | Grips rocky terrain/prey at close range | Developed forelimbs and traction | Not automatic excavation ability |
| GEN.SEN.POUNCER_FACIAL_FILAMENTS | Close-range whisker contact cues | Intact facial filaments | Contact range, damage |
| GEN.MOR.POUNCER_MOTTLED_COAT | Breaks silhouette in mineral/moss cover | Appropriate body covering | Camouflage is habitat-dependent |

**Inheritance:** all modules hypothetical heritable factors; none confirmed dominant/recessive. **Latent possibilities:** Rise versus Mossbank coat/foothold differences need population evidence. **Ecological role:** predator pressure on exposed prey, den shelters and carcass remains; no assumed large-scale restoration. **Absorption distinction:** motion-contrast eye evolution is a distinct research branch, not automatic universal sight. **Breeding:** pairings CX until mechanisms documented.

Seed movement via cached fruit is a hypothesis requiring actual behavior evidence. **Breeding:** CX with Moss-Horn and all unstudied species.

## 6. Restoration / counter-restoration feedback

The same organism can contribute to recovery or disturbance based on population density, resource availability, habitat state and season.

| Agent | Possible restoration effect | Counter-pressure | Required ecological evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moss-Horn Browser | Move viable seeds between feeding patches | Overbrowse shoots and seedlings | Seed survival, deposition, germination and browse density |
| Root-Crown Forager | Mix soil, expose nutrient-rich material, create small microhabitats | Uproot plants, disturb roots, destabilize banks | Soil condition, pit location, disturbance frequency |
| Glass-Eyed Pouncer | Indirectly change grazer distribution | Predation pressure may displace prey and alter grazing | Local prey behavior and food-web response |
| Mire Needle | Indirectly change wetland grazer activity | Sediment disturbance during lunges | Waterline disturbance and prey response |

No universal “restorer” or “destroyer” flag. Effects operate through local ecology and conservation of biomass; mature forests cannot regrow instantly because a restoration actor is present.

## 7. Hybrid design safeguards

- A Pouncer eye module cannot become functional in an eyeless recipient without a valid developmental/engineered evolution pathway; absorption and breeding are separate.
- Mire pressure pits and Pouncer eyes may coexist only in a future lineage with documented organs, development, metabolic cost and reproductive viability.
- Moss-Horn horns and Root-Crown tendrils are distinct cranial structures; occupying overlapping anatomy slots may create developmental conflicts.
- Forager digging and Browser mass could be costly or incompatible without supporting limbs and joints; no free maximal hybrid.
- Learned hunting strategies, herd responses and food caching are not assumed to be simple heritable genes.
- Any newly created hybrid remains an individual/lineage until persistent stable reproduction and species recognition requirements are satisfied.

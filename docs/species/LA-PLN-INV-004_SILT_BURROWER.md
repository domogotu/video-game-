# Lethal Absorption — Species Dossier: Silt Burrower

> **Stable ID:** LA-PLN-INV-004  
> **Planet:** Veyra  
> **Status:** Designed — concept dossier; not implemented or tested  
> **Primary region:** Reedwater Basin

## 1. Identity

**Role:** Sediment feeder, burrowing detritivore, and small-prey species.  
**Originality:** Original fictional invertebrate-like animal; all biology is a game-world proposal.

The Silt Burrower lives at the boundary between wet sediment and shallow water. Its blunt wedge-shaped head parts soft silt while a segmented body and short gripping limbs anchor it against current. It leaves small paired breathing holes and narrow trails of disturbed sediment.

## 2. Anatomy and life cycle

- **Body plan:** Compact segmented body, blunt digging head, short bracing limbs, sensory palps, and a flexible rear section used to reverse out of narrow tunnels.
- **Scale:** Small-to-medium prey relative to early Slime; exact dimensions depend on the finalized world scale.
- **Life stages:** Egg capsule in protected sediment → juvenile shallow burrower → adult tunnel-maker → senescent individual.
- **Reproduction:** Egg capsules placed in stable, oxygenated sediment. Flood scour can remove them; sediment compaction and pollution can reduce survival.
- **Diet:** Detritus, decomposing plant matter, microbial films, and small organic particles. It is not a large predator.
- **Mortality:** Predation, low oxygen, toxic sediment, prolonged exposure, and destructive scour.

## 3. Habitat and distribution

**Habitat anchors:** Reedwater Run banks, Reedglass Marsh, Siltbend Oxbows, and Southwash Fan's shallow depositional pools.

**Preferred conditions:** Moist, penetrable sediment with organic material, moderate flow, and periodic oxygen exchange.  
**Avoids or declines in:** Hard dry ground, deep unstable channels, severely compacted sediment, high contamination, and rapidly scoured banks.

A flood can expose or destroy burrows while depositing new sediment elsewhere. During drought, the species retreats into damp refuges only where depth and oxygen conditions remain viable; it must not survive arbitrary dry conditions without cost.

## 4. Senses and locomotion

- **Primary senses:** Touch and pressure through body and palps; chemical sampling of sediment and water; local vibration.
- **Movement:** Slow tunneling, braced crawling, short reverse retreats, and brief emergence to feed.
- **Limits:** Poor speed on exposed surfaces, limited endurance against strong current, and vulnerability while crossing open sediment.
- **Player clues:** Paired breathing holes, a fresh sediment fan, bubbles in still water, and subtle vibration close to the bank. These clues vary with current, substrate, and the Slime's available sensory modes.
- **Blind spots:** It does not know every surface predator's location. Strong current and sediment collapse can mask cues or interrupt its tunnel.

## 5. Behavior and AI contract

The Silt Burrower alternates between feeding, maintaining a shallow tunnel, sensing disturbance, retreating, and re-emerging after danger passes.

Minimum states: forage sediment → maintain tunnel → detect local disturbance → retreat deeper/sideways → wait → re-emerge. It should not teleport or remain invulnerable underground. Tunnel depth, sediment type, and disturbance strength determine whether it escapes or becomes exposed.

Population density is constrained by suitable sediment, organic material, oxygen, and predation. Tunnel creation moves sediment locally and can modestly increase water turbidity; it must not reshape the landscape at unrealistic speed.

## 6. Ecology and proposed links

- Processes detritus and microbial matter, returning nutrients to the sediment system.
- May consume or disturb Glassfilm-like microbial growth where suitable films are buried or adjacent; exact interaction remains unresolved.
- Serves as prey for wetland ambush predators and opportunistic aquatic hunters, but each predator's ability to excavate or capture it must be specified.
- Burrowing can improve local sediment mixing at small scales and increase turbidity after disturbance.
- Competition with other sediment feeders limits density where oxygen or detritus is scarce.

These links are proposed and not validated. Predator species must not be granted tunnel detection or excavation without a dossier-level rationale.

## 7. Resources and nonlethal study

- **Study:** Compare fresh tunnel traces, observe recovery after a disturbance, and examine how sediment condition changes its presence.
- **Possible resource:** A small sample of protective mucus or digestive residue only if a safe, nonlethal collection method is designed. No resource is guaranteed.
- **Harvest:** Digging up individuals or repeatedly disturbing burrows can reduce local population and damage sediment refuges.
- **Renewal:** Recovery depends on viable adults, egg survival, sediment quality, and recolonization. Pollution and compaction should persist until environmental conditions change.

## 8. Absorption and compatibility

Capture is conditional on Slime stage, size, access to the burrow, target condition, and the relevant capture capability. A target below the surface may be inaccessible without a valid sensing cue and a physical means of reaching it.

Potential learning hooks include local pressure interpretation, sediment-condition recognition, or a later burrowing-compatible movement adaptation. The Slime does not inherit the animal's entire segmented anatomy by eating it.

Potential risk: sediment contamination may expose the player to toxins. The cue system should communicate uncertainty rather than automatically certify the target as safe.

## 9. Skills and learning

The species cannot teach intentionally. Repeated observation may contribute to:
- **Substrate Pressure Reading:** Distinguish local tunnel activity from broad environmental vibration.
- **Sediment State Recognition:** Infer compaction, oxygen stress, and recent scour from visible/chemical clues.
- **Burrow-Escape Study:** Learn that prey may retreat out of sight without becoming invulnerable.

These are candidates pending integration into the skill atlas and playtesting.

## 10. Variants and discovery

Provisional forms: **Oxbow Silt Burrower** (slower, in more stable fine sediment) and **Runbank Silt Burrower** (more braced against intermittent current). These are regional forms, not separate species at present.

Discovery uses multiple cues—holes, sediment fans, bubbles, and local vibration—so players using different sensory views have viable information paths. Do not make a single tiny visual detail the only way to find the species.

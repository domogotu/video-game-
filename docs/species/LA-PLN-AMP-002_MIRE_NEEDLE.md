# Lethal Absorption — Species Dossier: Mire Needle

> **Stable ID:** LA-PLN-AMP-002  
> **Planet:** Veyra  
> **Status:** Designed — concept dossier; not implemented or tested  
> **Primary region:** Reedwater Basin's marsh channels and saturated margins

## 1. Identity and role

**Role:** Wetland ambush predator; creates pressure around shallow water, muddy crossings, and reed cover.  
**Originality:** Original fictional animal. “Needle” refers to its narrow strike profile and elongated snout, not a literal projectile.

The Mire Needle is a long-bodied, low-profile amphibious predator with a narrow head, lateral sensory pits, and a flexible tail. It lies almost motionless beneath reed shadows or at the edge of a shallow channel, then makes a short forward lunge. It is a waterline specialist rather than a fast open-water swimmer.

## 2. Anatomy and life stages

- **Body plan:** Elongated four-limbed amphibious body, webbed feet, flattened tail, narrow jaw, pressure-sensitive lateral pits, and a protective skin layer that reduces abrasion.
- **Scale:** Large enough to threaten small prey and an inexperienced Slime, but not an apex predator. Exact dimensions, health, and strike values await balancing.
- **Life stages:** Protected hatchling → juvenile channel hunter → adult ambush hunter → senescent adult.
- **Reproduction:** Egg masses or protected clutches in stable, oxygenated marsh margins. Flooding can disperse or smother eggs; drought can strand nursery habitat.
- **Diet:** Small wetland animals, suitable Reed Skippers, exposed sediment prey, and opportunistic aquatic prey when a plausible strike is available.
- **Mortality:** Desiccation, cold snaps, injury, prey scarcity, contamination, and larger predators.

## 3. Habitat and range

**Habitat anchors:** Reedglass Marsh, Reedwater Run's quiet margins, Siltbend Oxbows, and sheltered pools in Southwash Fan. It avoids broad, fast channels and open dry land.

**Preferred conditions:** Shallow water, muddy cover, submerged roots, stable ambush pockets, and short escape routes into water.  
**Poor conditions:** Dry exposed ground, strong currents, deep featureless water, and bare banks without cover.

During floods, it may abandon resting sites and redistribute to quieter side channels. During dry periods, it concentrates near persistent pools, but prey and oxygen can decline; a shrinking pool must not magically support unlimited predators.

## 4. Senses and movement

- **Primary detection:** Local water pressure changes, vibration through the substrate, close-range motion, and chemical cues carried through water.
- **Vision:** Useful for near-field contrast but not the main way it detects buried or obscured movement.
- **Movement:** Slow submerged positioning, tail-assisted short burst, low lunge at waterline, and brief retreat into cover.
- **Limits:** Poor sustained land travel, reduced strike control in deep fast water, and costly repositioning after a missed lunge.
- **Player-readable clues:** A slight waterline bulge, a narrow wake against current, disturbed silt, a sudden pause in small-animal activity, or repeated bubbles near a resting site. These cues vary with light, water clarity, and current.
- **Sensory fairness:** A Slime using vibration/chemical modes can infer a nearby disturbance but should not automatically receive an exact outline or species label.

## 5. Behavior and AI contract

Minimum states: submerged/rest → sample local flow → align with cover → wait for a plausible target → short lunge → secure prey or recover → retreat/reposition → rest. It does not pursue indefinitely onto dry land.

- Pressure cues are local and weaken with distance, turbulence, and disconnected substrate.
- The predator prioritizes feasible prey based on distance, size, exposure, and escape routes.
- If a lunge misses, it must pay a recovery/reposition cost before another attack.
- Disturbance or poor water conditions may trigger relocation.
- It can retreat from a much larger or persistently damaging opponent.

## 6. Ecology and proposed food-web links

- **Potential prey:** Reed Skipper near water, small exposed invertebrates, and Current Dart only if size and water conditions make capture plausible.
- **Possible interaction with Silt Burrower:** May catch an exposed individual near a burrow entrance; it cannot automatically detect or excavate deep tunnels.
- **Competition:** Overlaps with Reedstalker at some marsh edges, but differs in size, pursuit range, and preferred strike conditions.
- **Habitat effect:** Lunge and repositioning disturb small sediment patches; no large-scale landscape alteration.
- **Population feedback:** Suitable pools, prey density, oxygen, and nursery survival limit abundance. Crowding increases competition and unsuccessful strikes.

These links are provisional; exact predation relationships require ecology review and later simulation.

## 7. Resources and nonlethal study

- **Study:** Observe wake patterns, compare still and flowing water, map resting pockets, and note how it behaves when prey activity changes.
- **Nonlethal route:** Follow disturbance clues from the bank, observe from stable cover, and record activity across different water states.
- **Possible harvest:** A small sample only when a safe, compatible collection method exists; no automatic toxin or ability drop.
- **Harvest impact:** Repeated removal near one pool can disrupt local predator-prey balance. Renewal depends on survival, reproduction, and connected habitat.
- **Contamination:** The animal may accumulate environmental contaminants. Chemical richness is not proof of safety for absorption.

## 8. Capture, absorption, and compatibility

The Mire Needle is an active ambush threat. Capture requires a suitable player stage and approach, access to the animal's location, and relevant control/absorption mastery. Water can hinder or help the Slime depending on current, body condition, and unlocked adaptations.

Possible later learning branches:
- **Hydro-vibration Interpretation:** Better read local movement through connected water, with turbulence and substrate limits.
- **Waterline Threat Reading:** Infer ambush likelihood from wakes, bubbles, and prey silence.
- **Short-Burst Tail Propulsion Insight:** A possible aquatic movement adaptation only after compatibility review; does not grant a tail or full amphibious anatomy automatically.

The Slime must not gain exact radar, unlimited underwater breathing, or guaranteed immunity from a single absorption. Trait costs and incompatible structures remain explicit.

## 9. Skills and training

The species is non-sapient and cannot intentionally train. Repeated observation may contribute to:
- **Water Pressure Reading:** Distinguish nearby movement from current noise.
- **Ambush Pocket Mapping:** Learn which waterline shapes and cover arrangements support concealed attacks.
- **Lunge Recovery Timing:** Recognize that a missed strike creates a repositioning window.

These are learning hooks, not finalized skill unlocks. Each requires prerequisites, practice conditions, costs, mastery states, and counterplay.

## 10. Variants and discovery

Provisional regional forms:
- **Oxbow Mire Needle:** Favors still pools and submerged root cover; more vulnerable when water levels drop.
- **Run-edge Mire Needle:** More tolerant of intermittent flow but uses fewer deep ambush pockets.

These remain regional forms of one species. Discovery can begin through wake lines, silt disturbance, bubble trails, or shifts in nearby prey activity; no single cue is mandatory.

# Lethal Absorption — Species Dossier: Current Dart

> **Stable ID:** LA-PLN-AQU-001  
> **Planet:** Veyra  
> **Status:** Designed — concept dossier; not implemented or tested  
> **Primary region:** Reedwater Basin's flowing freshwater and connected oxbows

## 1. Identity and ecological role

**Role:** Small freshwater swimmer, mid-water grazer, and prey species.  
**Originality:** Original fictional aquatic animal; physiology and ecology are provisional worldbuilding.

The Current Dart is a narrow-bodied, finned freshwater animal adapted to maneuver in shallow currents and shelter behind stones, roots, and submerged stems. It feeds on drifting organic particles, small plankton-like organisms, and tiny invertebrates. Its movements give the player a readable indication of current direction and local water disturbance, but it is not a magical current detector.

## 2. Anatomy and life stages

- **Body plan:** Streamlined body, flexible tail fin, paired stabilizing fins, small sensory pores along the flanks, and a mouth suited to filtering or picking small food from the water.
- **Scale:** Small prey animal. Exact length, mass, health, and swimming speed await world scale and gameplay balance.
- **Life stages:** Egg/larval stage in protected shallows → juvenile in vegetated margins → adult in a broader range of flow conditions → senescent adult.
- **Reproduction:** Eggs deposited in sheltered, oxygenated shallows or attached to aquatic vegetation. Floods can disperse eggs; low oxygen and silt deposition can reduce survival. Exact clutch size and incubation remain unresolved.
- **Diet:** Drifting microbial/plankton-like food, fine organic particles, and tiny aquatic invertebrates.
- **Mortality:** Predation, poor oxygen, toxic runoff, extreme temperature shifts, and loss of connected shallow habitat.

## 3. Habitat and distribution

**Habitat anchors:** Reedwater Run, Siltbend Oxbows, Reedglass Marsh channels, and Southwash Fan pools when water quality and connectivity are suitable.

**Preferred conditions:** Oxygenated freshwater, moderate current, submerged cover, and connected nursery shallows.  
**Poor conditions:** Polluted pools, anoxic water, sudden temperature extremes, and exposed shallows with no cover.

During rising water, individuals may spread into newly connected margins. During falling water, they retreat toward persistent channels and pools; this increases crowding and predation risk. Disconnected pools cannot be assumed to replenish indefinitely from the river.

## 4. Senses and locomotion

- **Primary senses:** Lateral pressure/vibration sensing, chemical cues in water, and close-range vision.
- **Movement:** Continuous swimming, short rapid darts, station-holding behind cover, and brief bursts across open gaps.
- **Limits:** High current costs energy; stagnant or oxygen-poor water can be dangerous; a short burst does not provide sustained speed.
- **Player clues:** Directional ripples, synchronized darting, flashes of flank pattern at favorable angles, and localized disturbance near submerged roots.
- **Sensory fairness:** A Slime using chemical/vibration modes can infer movement in connected water, but should not receive exact coordinates or automatic species identification at unlimited range.

## 5. Behavior and AI contract

Minimum states: station-hold → forage drifting food → detect local disturbance → dart to cover or join a loose shoal → resume feeding. Shoal response is local and imperfect; one individual's cue does not grant every fish perfect awareness.

- Flow speed and direction influence station choice and movement cost.
- Individuals seek cover when alarm confidence rises, but can choose different escape paths.
- Strong current can separate a shoal; recovery takes time and requires viable habitat.
- Repeated disturbance can shift feeding patches.
- The species does not leap out of water or cross dry terrain without an explicitly designed mechanic.

## 6. Ecology and proposed food-web links

- Consumes small drifting food and aquatic microfauna; exact diet proportions remain open.
- May be prey for Mire Needle in suitable shallow strike conditions and for Reedstalker only if its hunting mechanics support aquatic capture.
- Its waste and uneaten food contribute to nutrient cycling; the scale of this effect must remain local.
- Shoal concentration can attract predators, but high density also depends on oxygen, food, and habitat connectivity.
- Glassfilm Microbe is not assumed to be directly eaten unless the microbe's ecology and the Current Dart's feeding mechanism support that link.

These are hypotheses for later ecology review and simulation, not verified food-web edges.

## 7. Resources and nonlethal study

- **Study:** Track shoal direction, compare movement in different flow rates, observe refuge use, and record seasonal nursery locations.
- **Nonlethal route:** Water observation and current mapping should offer meaningful progression without capture.
- **Possible resource:** A small sample only if a safe collection system and ecological cost are specified. No guaranteed skill drop or rare item.
- **Harvest impact:** Excess capture can reduce predator food supply and nursery recovery. Renewal depends on successful reproduction and connected habitat.
- **Water quality:** Contamination can harm the species; the presence of fish is not proof that water is safe.

## 8. Absorption and compatibility

Capture depends on player stage, relative size, access, stamina, water conditions, target health, and relevant control/absorption mastery. A fast dart may evade an unprepared Slime; it is not a guaranteed early meal.

Potential learning branches:
- **Flow-Vector Reading:** Better interpret local current direction from multiple cues.
- **Aquatic Vibration Interpretation:** Improve detection of movement through connected water, limited by turbulence and distance.
- **Short-Burst Glide Insight:** A possible later movement adaptation, subject to compatibility and cost review.

Absorption does not automatically grant gills, permanent aquatic breathing, a fish body, or unlimited swimming. No trait unlock is automatic.

## 9. Skills and training

The Current Dart cannot intentionally teach. Observation may contribute to:
- **Current Reading:** Infer local flow from ripples, debris, and animal positioning.
- **Shoal Disturbance Reading:** Distinguish coordinated escape from random water movement.
- **Aquatic Refuge Mapping:** Identify cover that reduces current cost and predator exposure.

These are candidates until formal skill records define prerequisites, practice, costs, mastery levels, and counterplay.

## 10. Variants and discovery

Provisional regional forms:
- **Run Current Dart:** More capable of station-holding in intermittent flow, but spends more energy in still water.
- **Oxbow Current Dart:** Better adapted to low-current cover, but more vulnerable when pools become oxygen-poor.

These remain regional forms of one species. Discovery may begin through ripples, moving debris, repeated flashes, or localized water disturbance; no single visual cue is mandatory.

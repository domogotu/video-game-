# Lethal Absorption — Species Dossier: Moss-Horn Browser

> **Stable ID:** LA-PLN-HER-001  
> **Planet:** Veyra  
> **Status:** Designed — concept dossier; not implemented or tested  
> **Primary region:** Reedwater Basin's temperate woodland and old-growth margins

## 1. Identity and role

**Role:** Medium herbivore, seed disperser, and habitat-shaping browser.  
**Originality:** Original fictional animal. Its paired moss-covered cranial growths are keratin-like display and defense structures, not living plant growth.

The Moss-Horn Browser moves through woodland edges and old-growth understory, feeding on leaves, shoots, soft bark, moss-associated growth, and seasonal fruit. Its slow, deliberate feeding helps players read the landscape: clipped branches, pressed vegetation, droppings, and disturbed moss indicate a recent route.

## 2. Anatomy and life stages

- **Body plan:** Four sturdy limbs, flexible neck, broad muzzle, mobile ears, thick hide, and paired backward-curving horn structures often coated with moss and lichen.
- **Scale:** Medium herbivore; larger than small prey and early Slime. Exact mass, height, and combat values await world-scale and balance decisions.
- **Life stages:** Hidden calf → dependent juvenile → subadult → mature browser → senescent adult. Young remain near protective cover and adults.
- **Reproduction:** One or occasionally more offspring per reproductive event is an open design choice; seasonal breeding is tied to food and shelter, not a fixed guaranteed spawn.
- **Diet:** Leaves, shoots, soft bark, fallen fruit, and selected moss-associated growth. It does not consume every plant indiscriminately.
- **Mortality:** Predation on young, disease, injury, drought-driven food shortage, habitat fragmentation, and age-related decline.

## 3. Habitat and range

**Habitat anchors:** Mossbank Edge, Elderbank Stand, First Drip Hollow's outer woodland, and sheltered Longgrass Shelf tree lines.

**Preferred conditions:** Mixed-age woodland, edible understory, mineral access, nearby water, and connected cover.  
**Poor conditions:** Bare rock, deep flooded ground, heavily disturbed clearings, and long stretches without browse.

Seasonal growth changes its feeding routes. Flooding pushes groups to higher ground; drought reduces tender shoots and can lengthen travel between feeding patches. The species should not remain abundant when the habitat loses its food base.

## 4. Senses, movement, and defense

- **Senses:** Broad vision, acute hearing, smell, and ground vibration through the limbs. Dense cover and wind can confuse specific cues.
- **Movement:** Steady walk, short defensive rush, careful stepping over roots, and group withdrawal through familiar corridors.
- **Defense:** Threat display, head lowering, horn sweep at close range, and retreat if a safe route remains.
- **Limits:** Poor turning in dense root tangles, high cost of repeated charges, and reduced footing on saturated slopes.
- **Player clues:** Fresh browse marks, snapped tender shoots, hoof-like tracks, moss rubbed from horn structures, low calls, and branches moving above the Slime's immediate line of sight.
- **Fairness:** Warning postures and escape routes should be legible through more than color; a Slime relying on chemical/vibration cues can infer nearby mass and movement without receiving exact visual identification.

## 5. Behavior and AI contract

Moss-Horn Browsers form small, flexible groups around feeding areas. Adults are vigilant while feeding, and group members can react to one another's alarm cues without acting as a perfect shared sensor network.

Minimum states: browse → scan/listen → follow feeding route → alarm → group withdrawal → defensive display/short rush if cornered → settle after danger passes. Calves prioritize cover; adults may interpose themselves but should not fight to the death by default.

- Alarm confidence depends on visible movement, sound, scent, and proximity.
- The herd should choose routes based on terrain, food, and group safety.
- Crowding and repeated disturbance can displace the group to another patch.
- No teleportation, instant group-wide awareness, or infinite defensive stamina.

## 6. Ecology and proposed food-web links

- Browses understory vegetation and may disperse seeds through movement and waste.
- Creates small trails and feeding gaps that alter plant structure over time.
- Competes with Root-Crown Forager for some roots, fallen fruit, and soft plant material, while differing in foraging height and digging behavior.
- Young or injured individuals may be prey for larger predators only where predator size, group defense, and terrain make it plausible.
- Droppings and discarded plant matter support decomposers and small detritivores.

All edges are ecological hypotheses until formal review and simulation.

## 7. Resources and nonlethal study

- **Study:** Follow browse lines, map seasonal feeding routes, observe alarm calls from cover, and compare disturbed and undisturbed woodland patches.
- **Nonlethal learning:** Tracking and behavior observation should reveal useful ecology without requiring a kill.
- **Possible resources:** Shed horn material, naturally dropped hair, or a small safe sample only if resource rules support it. Do not require killing an adult to access basic ecological knowledge.
- **Harvest impact:** Repeatedly removing adults can reduce seed dispersal and alter browsing pressure. Recovery depends on reproduction, forage, and connected habitat.
- **Renewal:** Horn coatings and shed material renew slowly; they must not be infinitely harvestable on demand.

## 8. Absorption and compatibility

The Moss-Horn Browser is not an early guaranteed combat target. Capture depends on Slime stage, size, available control and defense, group context, stamina, target condition, and mastery. A herd may withdraw before a capture opportunity appears.

Potential learning branches:
- **Browse-Trace Interpretation:** Read plant damage and feeding routes.
- **Mass-Shift Warning:** Interpret heavy movement and defensive posture through ground vibration.
- **Horn-Guard Insight:** A possible later defensive adaptation, subject to anatomy compatibility and a defined load/capacity cost.

Absorption does not automatically give the Slime horns, herbivore digestion, quadruped movement, or group coordination. Every adaptation requires compatibility review, analysis, practice, and mastery.

## 9. Skills and training

The species cannot intentionally train. Observation may contribute to:
- **Woodland Tracking:** Distinguish fresh browse and travel traces from old damage.
- **Herd Alarm Reading:** Infer when a group has detected a possible threat.
- **Seed-Route Ecology:** Learn how large herbivores connect feeding areas and plant regeneration.

These are candidates, not finalized skills. Formal skill entries must define prerequisites, training conditions, costs, mastery stages, and counterplay.

## 10. Variants and discovery

Provisional regional forms:
- **Elderbank Moss-Horn:** Larger coating of moss/lichen and more use of old-growth cover.
- **Longgrass Edge Browser:** More seasonal movement between woodland and grassland, with less dense horn coating.

These are regional forms of one species unless later evidence supports a taxonomic split. Discovery can begin through browse marks, tracks, calls, and disturbed vegetation.

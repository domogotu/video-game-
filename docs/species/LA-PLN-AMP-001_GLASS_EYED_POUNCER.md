# Lethal Absorption — Species Dossier: Glass-Eyed Pouncer

> **Stable ID:** LA-PLN-AMP-001  
> **Planet:** Veyra  
> **Status:** Designed — concept dossier; not implemented or tested  
> **Primary region:** Reedwater Basin's rocky woodland and scrub margins

## 1. Identity and role

**Role:** Small ambush predator; pressure on exposed small prey and an early lesson in concealment and sensory uncertainty.  
**Originality:** Original fictional animal. The name refers to its reflective, light-sensitive eyes, not transparent glass or supernatural detection.

The Glass-Eyed Pouncer is a compact, low-slung predator with a mottled mineral-and-moss coat, large reflective eyes, and powerful hindquarters. It waits near stones, root tangles, and dry pockets beside damp cover, then makes one short explosive leap. It is dangerous when it chooses the engagement, but poor at prolonged pursuit.

## 2. Anatomy and life stages

- **Body plan:** Four-limbed vertebrate-like quadruped; flexible spine, broad hind limbs, gripping foreclaws, mobile ears, whisker-like facial filaments, and paired image-forming eyes.
- **Scale:** Small predator relative to the starting Slime; exact dimensions, mass, health, and damage remain unassigned pending world scale and balance.
- **Life stages:** Den-bound juvenile → learning subadult → territorial adult → senescent adult. Juveniles hunt only very small prey and remain near sheltered cover.
- **Reproduction:** Small concealed litter in a dry den above ordinary flood reach. Litter size, gestation, and breeding frequency remain open design variables.
- **Diet:** Small surface-active prey, including suitable Reed Skippers and other small animals where the capture opportunity is plausible.
- **Mortality:** Starvation, injury, larger predators, den flooding, and loss of suitable cover. It avoids costly fights.

## 3. Habitat and range

**Habitat anchors:** Flintback Rise, Mossbank Edge, Longgrass Shelf's rocky margins, and dry root tangles around the basin. It can approach marsh edges but does not specialize in deep water.

**Preferred conditions:** Broken sightlines, stable footholds, dry den sites, and prey routes that pass close to cover.  
**Poor conditions:** Open water, deep mud, broad exposed plains, and long stretches of dense vegetation that obstruct its leap.

Seasonal flooding can displace den sites and compress hunting territory onto higher ground. During heavy rain, scent trails become less reliable and leap footing may deteriorate. Population density depends on den availability and prey abundance.

## 4. Senses and movement

- **Vision:** Strong low-light contrast and motion sensitivity; glare, opaque cover, and abrupt changes between bright and dark areas can reduce reliability. Its eyes are a distinct species-specific visual design, not a universal template for every eye-bearing species.
- **Hearing and touch:** Detects nearby movement through sound and whisker contact. Wind, flowing water, and environmental noise can mask small prey.
- **Chemical cues:** Uses scent for territory and close-range tracking, but does not automatically follow every chemical trail.
- **Movement:** Quiet stalk, short crouch, explosive pounce, brief scramble over rocks, and retreat to cover.
- **Limits:** Low endurance in pursuit, vulnerable landing recovery after a missed leap, and reduced traction on slick stone or deep mud.
- **Player-readable clues:** Brief eye-shine at a suitable angle, a compressed stance, displaced grit, claw marks, or small prey alarm movements. Eye-shine is conditional and must not make the predator always visible.

## 5. Behavior and AI contract

Behavior is based on local evidence and an energy budget, not omniscient player tracking.

Minimum states: shelter/rest → patrol cover edge → detect possible prey → stalk → pounce → secure prey or recover from miss → feed → retreat/clean → return to cover. A threat response can interrupt hunting. Repeated failed pounces should increase caution or trigger relocation rather than infinite attacks.

- Detection confidence combines motion, sound, scent, and line of sight; each cue can be disrupted.
- The predator should not target the player through opaque walls without a valid cue.
- It can abandon a chase when stamina or risk crosses a threshold.
- A missed pounce creates a readable recovery interval but should not guarantee a free kill.
- Territorial displays and retreat signals precede escalation when distance permits.

## 6. Ecology and proposed food-web links

- **Potential prey:** Reed Skipper and other appropriately sized surface-active animals. The exact predation rate is not fixed.
- **Competition:** Competes with Mire Needle only in overlapping prey opportunities; the two predators use different terrain and hunting methods.
- **Environmental effect:** Abandoned dens and discarded prey remains can support scavengers and decomposers.
- **Population feedback:** Low prey density reduces successful feeding and reproduction; high predator density increases territory overlap and failed hunts.
- **Avoided assumption:** It is not assumed to excavate Silt Burrower tunnels or detect buried prey without a specified cue or action.

All food-web edges are design hypotheses until reviewed and validated through ecological simulation.

## 7. Resources and nonlethal study

- **Study:** Record pounce distance, approach posture, eye-shine conditions, missed-attack recovery, and the terrain where it abandons a chase.
- **Nonlethal route:** Observe from cover, use environmental decoys if later supported by the game systems, or investigate den traces without entering a den.
- **Possible harvest:** A small biological sample only after a safe collection mechanic is established. No guaranteed rare drop.
- **Renewal:** Removing adults or disturbing dens can reduce local predator numbers; replacement depends on prey, den sites, and successful reproduction.
- **Safety feedback:** Its posture, distance, and recent attack state should communicate escalating danger without relying on color alone.

## 8. Capture, absorption, and trait compatibility

The Glass-Eyed Pouncer is a live predator, not a passive resource. Capture depends on the player's stage, size, available control/defense, stamina, target condition, and relevant mastery. Its pounce can interrupt an unprepared approach.

Possible later learning branches:
- **Motion Contrast Vision:** Improved interpretation of movement against complex backgrounds, with glare and darkness trade-offs.
- **Explosive Launch Mechanics:** A limited short-burst movement insight, not automatic transfer of quadruped limbs or full pounce strength.
- **Predator Posture Reading:** Learn to infer imminent attack from stance and weight shift.

The eye-derived branch must remain distinct from other species' sensory adaptations. Absorbing this species does not grant perfect night vision, x-ray sight, universal target outlines, or its complete anatomy. No ability unlock is automatic.

## 9. Skills, training, and mastery

The animal cannot intentionally train the player. Repeated observation or compatible analysis may contribute to:
- **Ambush Cue Recognition:** Detect posture and terrain cues that precede a pounce.
- **Motion-Contrast Interpretation:** Learn the strengths and blind spots of a motion-sensitive visual system.
- **Pounce Recovery Timing:** Understand the predator's post-leap recovery window without guaranteeing an attack opportunity.

Each remains a candidate until linked to a skill record with prerequisites, practice conditions, mastery levels, costs, and counterplay.

## 10. Variants and discovery

Provisional regional forms:
- **Rise Pouncer:** Better foothold use on dry rock; poorer performance in mud.
- **Mossbank Pouncer:** More mottled coat and greater use of root cover; eye-shine may be less obvious through foliage.

These are regional variants of one species, not separate IDs or finalized stat variants. Discovery may begin through tracks, prey alarm, displaced grit, or an interrupted feeding site.

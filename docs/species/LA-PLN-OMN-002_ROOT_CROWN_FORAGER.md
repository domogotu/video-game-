# Lethal Absorption — Species Dossier: Root-Crown Forager

> **Stable ID:** LA-PLN-OMN-002  
> **Planet:** Veyra  
> **Status:** Designed — concept dossier; not implemented or tested  
> **Primary region:** Reedwater Basin's woodland, grassland edges, and root-rich ground

## 1. Identity and role

**Role:** Burrowing omnivore and small-scale soil engineer.  
**Originality:** Original fictional animal; its root-like crown is a sensory and protective structure, not a plant growing from its skull.

The Root-Crown Forager noses through leaf litter and soft ground, using a crown of stiff sensory tendrils around its blunt snout to locate edible roots, fungi-like growths, insects, and fallen fruit. It digs shallow feeding pits and reuses burrow systems when conditions are safe.

## 2. Anatomy and life stages

- **Body plan:** Compact four-limbed mammal-like omnivore, reinforced forelimbs, blunt digging snout, flexible back, short protective tail, and a ring of root-shaped keratinous sensory tendrils around the muzzle.
- **Scale:** Small-to-medium; capable of displacing soft soil but not tunneling through solid rock. Exact dimensions and mass remain open.
- **Life stages:** Nestling → juvenile digger → independent forager → mature burrow-holder → senescent adult.
- **Reproduction:** Protected nest chamber with seasonal breeding linked to food availability. Litter size and gestation remain unresolved.
- **Diet:** Soft roots, fallen fruit, fungi-like nutrient bodies, invertebrates, carrion scraps, and organic-rich soil. It does not eat mineral soil as a complete food source.
- **Mortality:** Predation, tunnel collapse, drought, low food availability, contamination, and injury during digging.

## 3. Habitat and distribution

**Habitat anchors:** Mossbank Edge, Elderbank Stand, Longgrass Shelf, Siltbend Oxbows' raised banks, and the outer root labyrinth near Quietroot Grotto.

**Preferred conditions:** Loose but stable soil, root networks, leaf litter, seasonal food, and nearby shelter.  
**Poor conditions:** Bare rock, waterlogged collapsing ground, compacted soil, severe drought, and frequently scoured banks.

The forager shifts feeding pits as local food is depleted. Floods may collapse low burrows and move activity to higher ground; drought hardens soil and raises digging costs. Population depends on recoverable food and safe nesting ground.

## 4. Senses, movement, and defense

- **Senses:** Smell, touch through the muzzle crown, close-range chemical sampling, hearing, and local vibration. The tendrils are not a supernatural radar.
- **Movement:** Quick quadrupedal scurry, short digging bursts, backing into a burrow, and cautious surface crossings.
- **Defense:** Retreat, kick loose soil, use a narrow burrow entrance, and bite only at close range if cornered.
- **Limits:** Slow digging in compact or saturated soil, poor speed across open ground, and vulnerability if a burrow floods or collapses.
- **Player clues:** Fresh soil fans, small paired tracks, root fragments, repeated sniffing movements, and soft scraping beneath loose ground.
- **Fairness:** It cannot identify a Slime through arbitrary layers of earth. Pressure or vibration cues are local and depend on connected substrate and distance.

## 5. Behavior and AI contract

The Forager alternates between shallow digging, surface feeding, carrying food to shelter, resting, and checking nearby disturbances.

Minimum states: scent/inspect → forage → dig shallow pit → collect food → feed or cache → detect disturbance → retreat to burrow/cover → wait → resume. Food caching should be bounded and visible through clues. Burrows are refuges, not invulnerable safe rooms.

- Digging speed depends on soil type, moisture, fatigue, and forelimb condition.
- Repeated disturbance can move the animal's home range.
- It avoids unnecessary fights and may abandon a food cache when threatened.
- Group behavior is limited to compatible family or shared shelter contexts, not a coordinated colony.

## 6. Ecology and proposed food-web links

- Consumes roots, fallen fruit, fungi-like growth, invertebrates, and some detritus; diet shifts with season.
- Moves soil and exposes small patches of buried organic material, influencing local nutrient cycling.
- May disturb or consume microbial films where they occur on exposed roots or soil surfaces; this interaction requires review.
- Competes with Moss-Horn Browser for some fallen fruit and soft plant resources, but uses more below-ground foods.
- Can be prey for a predator only when body size, burrow access, and hunting behavior support the encounter.

No predator is assumed to excavate the Forager's burrow without a specific ability and cost. Food-web edges remain hypotheses until tested.

## 7. Resources and nonlethal study

- **Study:** Track digging patterns, compare pits in different soils, observe food caching, and document burrow relocation after flooding.
- **Nonlethal learning:** Soil disturbance and food remains provide ecological information without capturing the animal.
- **Possible resources:** Naturally shed sensory tendril material or a safe, small sample only if a harvesting system is established. Do not turn the animal into an unlimited source of rare materials.
- **Harvest impact:** Repeatedly destroying burrows removes shelter and can suppress local populations; digging also affects other organisms using the same ground.
- **Renewal:** Soil structure, food abundance, and burrow recovery change over time and cannot be reset instantly.

## 8. Absorption and compatibility

Capture is conditional on Slime stage, relative size, target access, stamina, burrow geometry, and the relevant control ability. A hidden animal should first be inferred through valid cues; no exact underground outline is granted automatically.

Potential learning branches:
- **Substrate Disturbance Reading:** Interpret fresh digging traces and local ground changes.
- **Root-Chemistry Recognition:** Improve identification of some edible-root and organic nutrient cues, with contamination and species variation as limits.
- **Soil-Pressure Adaptation:** A possible later movement or sensing branch only if compatible anatomy and resource costs are defined.

The Slime does not inherit a quadruped body, instant digging claws, omnivorous digestion, or the animal's entire sensory system by consuming it.

## 9. Skills and training

The Forager cannot intentionally teach. Repeated observation may contribute to:
- **Burrow Trace Reading:** Distinguish recent activity from abandoned tunnels.
- **Soil Condition Assessment:** Infer compaction, saturation, and collapse risk from multiple clues.
- **Forage Patch Mapping:** Learn how seasonal food availability changes animal activity.

These are learning candidates until formal skill records specify prerequisites, practice, costs, mastery, and counterplay.

## 10. Variants and discovery

Provisional regional forms:
- **Elderbank Root-Crown:** More use of thick root networks and fallen fruit.
- **Longgrass Root-Crown:** More digging in open, looser soil and greater exposure to surface predators.

They remain regional forms of one species. Discovery begins with soil fans, feeding pits, tracks, and muffled scraping; none should be the sole cue.

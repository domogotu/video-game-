# Lethal Absorption — Species Dossier: Reed Skipper

> **Stable ID:** LA-PLN-INV-001  
> **Planet:** Veyra  
> **Status:** Designed — concept dossier; not implemented or tested  
> **Primary region:** Reedwater Basin

## 1. Identity

**Role:** Small wetland grazer and prey species.  
**Originality:** Original fictional invertebrate-like animal; visual and ecological details are provisional.

The Reed Skipper is a small, low-bodied marsh animal with a flexible plated back, broad hind limbs, and fine sensory filaments along its head and flanks. It darts between reed bases and floating plant debris. Its name describes its rapid surface-to-surface movement; it is not a fish and does not fly.

## 2. Anatomy and life stages

- **Body plan:** Six short support limbs, enlarged springing hind pair, low flexible abdomen, overlapping moisture-retaining plates, and sensory filaments.
- **Size:** Small enough to be plausible prey for early predators, but too large for a Stage 0 cell to absorb. Exact dimensions and mass await scale and combat tuning.
- **Life stages:** Egg cluster attached beneath protected stems → juvenile → adult → senescent adult. Juveniles remain nearer dense cover; adults forage across small wetland patches.
- **Reproduction:** Seasonal egg clusters in sheltered reed bases. Flooding can disperse or destroy clusters; no exact clutch size or incubation time is established.
- **Diet:** Scrapes microbial films, soft plant growth, and fine organic matter from submerged stems and stones.
- **Mortality:** Predation, desiccation, severe pollution, freezing exposure, and habitat loss. It can retreat into cover rather than fight.

## 3. Habitat and range

**Primary habitat instances:** Reedglass Marsh, Reedwater Run margins, Siltbend Oxbows, and Southwash Fan pools.  
**Preferred conditions:** Shallow water, stable reed cover, damp surfaces, and nearby microbial films.  
**Poor conditions:** Bare exposed stone, deep fast channels, long dry periods, and heavily disturbed or contaminated water.

Seasonal floods temporarily open dispersal routes but can wash juveniles out of protected nursery areas. During dry spells, individuals concentrate in remaining pools and become easier for predators to locate. These changes should emerge from water state and habitat cover, not fixed calendar spawns alone.

## 4. Senses and movement

- **Primary senses:** Short-range chemical sampling, substrate vibration, touch through sensory filaments, and limited broad light/dark discrimination.
- **Movement:** Rapid short hops, scuttling, brief surface skims, and retreat under stems or loose plant litter.
- **Limits:** Poor endurance in open water; cannot cross wide dry gaps reliably; damaged hind limbs reduce escape distance.
- **Player-readable cues:** Reed tips flick, water dimples, and repeated tiny contact sounds before a nearby individual becomes visible. A slime relying on chemical/vibration views can detect clues but should not receive automatic species identification.
- **Counterplay:** Slow approach, use of cover, approaching from up-current to manage scent trails, or waiting for a safe observation opportunity. Hard pursuit through open mud is inefficient.

## 5. Behavior and AI contract

Default behavior is cautious foraging in a small home range. Individuals pause to scrape surfaces, groom sensory filaments, and return to cover after disturbances.

Minimum states: forage → pause/listen → evade → hide → resume feeding → juvenile shelter. A high-confidence nearby predator cue triggers evasion; uncertain cues cause a pause or movement toward cover rather than a perfect escape response. The animal should not know the player's exact position without a valid cue.

Group behavior is loose aggregation around suitable feeding patches, not a coordinated hive. Density should be limited by food availability, shelter, and seasonal survival.

## 6. Ecology and proposed food-web links

- Grazes Glassfilm-like microbial films and soft plant growth; this link remains a design hypothesis pending review.
- Converts microbial/plant biomass into prey biomass for small ambush predators.
- Disturbs fine surface material while feeding and may help move small organic particles between stems and shallow water.
- Competes with other small grazers when suitable film is scarce.
- Floods may disperse individuals while reducing local abundance; dry periods concentrate rather than create new biomass.

No food-web link should be treated as simulation-validated until recorded by a future ecology test.

## 7. Resources and study

- **Nonlethal study:** Track feeding marks, observe escape direction, compare active and dry-period shelter use, and document juvenile nursery stems.
- **Possible resource:** Small biological sample after the player gains an appropriate safe collection method. No guaranteed ability drop.
- **Harvest rule:** Over-collection reduces local prey density and can affect predator presence. The population must recover through survival and reproduction, not instant respawn.
- **Ethical route:** Observation and environmental clues should provide useful learning even when the player chooses not to absorb the animal.

## 8. Absorption and evolution compatibility

The Reed Skipper is a small live organism, but successful capture depends on player stage, relative size, stamina, target condition, and the relevant capture/absorption capability. It is not guaranteed prey.

Potential adaptations are limited to later compatibility review: improved short-burst movement, tactile filament interpretation, or moisture-retention cues. These are not granted simply by consuming a specimen. Its limb anatomy does not automatically transfer as functional limbs to the limbless Slime.

Possible hazards include defensive secretion from stressed adults and contamination from polluted habitat. Exact toxicity and dose are unresolved; avoid arbitrary instant-kill effects.

## 9. Skills and trainers

The species is not sapient and cannot intentionally train the player. Repeated observation may contribute to:
- **Wetland Motion Reading:** Learn to interpret reed flicks and small water disturbances.
- **Burst-Escape Analysis:** Learn how short-hop prey use cover and direction changes.
- **Micrograzer Ecology:** Learn the relationship between surface films, shallow-water prey, and habitat condition.

These are learning hooks only; each requires a formal skill record, prerequisites, mastery progression, and validation.

## 10. Variants and discovery

Provisional ecological forms: **Oxbow Reed Skipper** (more tolerant of still, warm pools) and **Run-edge Reed Skipper** (more practiced at avoiding intermittent current). They are regional forms of one species unless future evidence warrants a split.

Discovery should begin with indirect cues—moving reeds, fresh scraping marks, or tiny surface dimples—before the animal is fully revealed. Avoid relying on color alone.
